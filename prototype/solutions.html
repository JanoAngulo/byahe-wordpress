Para sa bawat negosyo.

For every kind of business. A sari-sari store, a café, a two-person studio and an online shop all get paid differently. Here's how BYAHE fits each one.

  • Sari-sari and retail
  • Food and cafés
  • Freelancers and agencies
  • Online sellers

Sari-sari and retail.

The headache: hunting for barya, and a notebook full of utang nobody wants to bring up.

  • A printed QR Ph code at the counter, no card terminal needed
  • Suki credit tracked as small invoices, with gentle reminders
  • One sales total at closing time, not a shoebox of receipts

Food and cafés.

The headache: a long line at the counter while someone waits for change, and a cash count that never matches.

  • A QR code on every table or receipt, so guests pay without the line
  • Split the bill by person, paid by QR, card or e-wallet
  • An end-of-day report by payment method

Freelancers and agencies.

The headache: chasing clients thirty days after the work is done, and rebuilding the same invoice every month.

  • Retainer invoices that send themselves on the same day each month
  • Deposits and milestone billing for project work
  • Clients abroad pay by card, and you receive pesos

Online sellers.

The headache: “Paid na po” screenshots to check one by one, and orders that go quiet after the chat.

  • A payment link for each order, sent right in the chat
  • Orders marked paid the moment the money clears, no screenshots
  • Unpaid links expire, so stock goes back on sale
